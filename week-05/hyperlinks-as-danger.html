Hyperlinks as Danger

When you post a URL in an Instagram, tiktok, facebook comment, it appears as plain text rather than a clickable hyperlink. Users must manually copy the URL and paste it in. It is to Reduce spam and malicious content, Keep users on the platform, and reserving link privileges for specific areas of the platform, encouraging businesses to use features like Instagram Shopping, ads, or the coveted “link in bio.” There are “malicious links” URLs that lead somewhere harmful, such as a fake login page, a malware download, a payment scam or a fraudulent support line. The link itself is just the entry point. An attacker sends a believable lure, like a shared document, an invoice, a package alert or an account warning. They hide the real destination behind a button, a shortened URL, a QR code, a lookalike domain or a chain of redirects. The page then asks for credentials, an MFA code, payment, a download or a phone call, and the attacker uses whatever they get to take over accounts, commit fraud or spread further. Clicking links is routine, so it is a very easy trap to fall for.

clicking a link means trusting but also somewhat not knowing where it leads. Artists use that to make unpredictable, choose-your-own-path work, while scammers exploit it. Non-linear reading lets readers pick their own order and build their own meaning, but that is also limited by what is made available. I think in both real life and hypertext art, clicking a link involves a risk similar to how in The Garden of Forking Paths, Yu Tsun’s chosen paths always lead to alternative possibilities.
